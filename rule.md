# Creative Direction & Editorial Standard: Existential Horror

## 1. The Anti-Trope Banned Lexicon

Never use melodramatic, generic AI horror adjectives. Under no circumstances should any script, prompt, or beat contain:

- BANNED WORDS: "Chilling", "spine-tingling", "blood-curdling", "unfathomable", "nightmarish", "eldritch", "sinister", "eerie", "epic cinematic", "hyperrealistic".
- BANNED CONCEPTS: No jump scares, no red glowing eyes, no shadowy figures lurking in corners, no maniacal laughing.

## 2. The Sensory Grounding Law (Physicality Over Metaphor)

Never describe abstract fear ("She felt immense terror"). Horror must be communicated purely through mechanical, tactile anatomy and sound:

- Mandatory: Focus on involuntary physiological reactions: enamel scraping against enamel, sweat stinging an open tear duct, cold fat congealing on the tongue, tendons straining in the forearm.
- Every shot variable must specify the exact light source (e.g., "single 40W tungsten bulb 3 feet overhead", not "dark atmospheric lighting").

## 3. Cinematographic Rigor

Prompt visual models like a technical director of photography, not a spectator:

- Always specify lens focal length (e.g., 28mm wide for claustrophobia, 85mm shallow depth-of-field for isolation).
- Specify explicit camera height and tilt (e.g., "low-angle knee-height looking up at 25 degrees", "high-angle Dutch tilt 15 degrees counter-clockwise").
- Emphasize texture and print artifacts: "heavy Indian ink line art, cel-shading, 35mm film grain, micro-scratches on emulsion".

## 4. Audio-First Pacing Guardrail

When structuring shot lists, sound design must dictate the cut. Never propose an action without pairing it to a hyper-specific foley stem (e.g., "the wet tear of cardboard", "the hollow vacuum pop of an air seal").
